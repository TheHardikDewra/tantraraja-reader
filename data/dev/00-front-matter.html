About this edition

This is a personal reading edition, made for an e-reader. It holds all 36 paṭalas (chapters) of the Tantrarājatantra with the Manoramā commentary, in the order of the printed book.

The printed original. Arthur Avalon (Sir John Woodroffe), ed., Tantrik Texts. Part 1 (vol. 8): paṭalas 1 to 18, edited by Mahāmahopādhyāya Lakṣmaṇa Śāstrī. Part 2 (vol. 12, 1926): paṭalas 19 to 36, edited by Sadāśiva Miśra. Part 2's page numbers run on from Part 1. The title page in the e-text says it was printed in Calcutta at the Mahāmāyā Press and published in London by Luzac & Co. The text is Sanskrit. The commentary is printed under the root text.

The typed text used here. Muktabodha Indological Research Institute, e-text M00122, "tantrarājatantra with commentary manorama". Transcribed by the staff of Muktabodha under the supervision of Mark S. G. Dyczkowski. Revision 0, 17 Sept 2008. The file names the publisher as Tantrik Text Series and the year as 1929; the archive.org scans of Part 1 give 1918 or 1919. Downloaded from the Muktabodha Digital Library: https://muktalib7.com/DL_CATALOG_ROOT/digital_library.htm

How the pages are laid out

  • Root text (mūla). Each verse is a bold, indented block. Each line is one half-verse, as in the e-text. The verse number ends the second line, between double bars, like this: || १ ||.
  • Commentary (Manoramā). Normal paragraphs. Each run of commentary starts with a small label: मनोरमा.
  • Page numbers. Markers like प्. १९) are the page numbers of the printed book, kept from the e-text.
  • Chapter titles. "पटलः १", "पटलः २" and so on are navigation labels added for this edition. The chapter heading under it is the one in the e-text.
  • One repair, from Muktabodha's own master. Muktabodha publishes this e-text in two scripts, IAST and Devanagari. Their Devanagari copy has a conversion bug: in 539 lines a virama (्) is lost or misplaced, so it prints र्यय for र्य्य (kuryyāt) and यव for य्व (vāyvādi). Those lines were rebuilt from Muktabodha's IAST copy, and only where the virama was the sole difference. Two English editorial notes that the conversion had turned into nonsense Devanagari are shown in English, in square brackets. Every repaired line is listed in qa/dev-fixes.tsv.
  • No other changes to the words. Every Devanagari letter comes from the Muktabodha file. A script checked that each chapter holds exactly the same letters, in the same order, as its slice of the file. Spellings, typos and editorial marks of the e-text (brackets, question marks, slashes) are kept as they are. The only layout change: commentary lines, which the e-text breaks at a fixed width, are joined into paragraphs.
  • Scrambled verses (vyākulitākṣara). Some passages are printed on purpose with their syllables out of order, so that they are not read by the uninitiated. This happens in paṭala 8 (around verses 72 to 90, with part of their commentary) and in paṭala 35 (a few verses near the start, and some later ones). They are kept exactly as the source prints them. Do not read them as typos.
  • The IAST edition is made from this Devanagari text by a transliteration library (indic_transliteration), not typed by hand.

Who wrote the commentary

The closing verses of the commentary say that Subhagānandanātha wrote the Manoramā up to paṭala 22, and that his disciple Prakāśānanda wrote it for the remaining 14 paṭalas:

शुभगानन्दनाथेन द्वाविंशपटलावधि |
तदाज्ञयावशिष्टानां पटलानां विनिर्ममे ||
चतुर्द्दशानां तच्छिष्यः प्रकाशानन्ददेशिकः |

Verse numbering notes

Each paṭala has about 100 verses, as the commentary itself says (श्लोकशतरूपेण). A script listed the verse numbers in the root text of every chapter. Where a number does not appear, the reason is below.

  • Paṭala 4, verse numbers 22: The number is printed with one bar in front (| २२ ||). The verse is there.
  • Paṭala 4, verse numbers 69: Missing in the source. The Muktabodha typists noted: "śloka 69 missing in Avalon edition?" Nothing was added in its place. Near verse 78 the typists also noted "so one of the lines between this numbered incorrectly".
  • Paṭala 6, verse numbers 35 to 40: In the e-text these six verses sit inside a commentary block with no separator. They are shown here as root verses.
  • Paṭala 7, verse numbers 26: Printed with one bar in front of the number. The verse is there.
  • Paṭala 8, verse numbers 72, 73 and 75 to 89: The scrambled verses (vyākulitākṣara). They are printed without numbers; only 74 and 90 carry one. The commentary itself says verses 73 to 90 are scrambled. All the text is there, kept as printed. Woodroffe's Short Analysis also names verses 72 to 90 as the scrambled ones.
  • Paṭala 11, verse numbers 87 and 90: Printed with a broken bar (| ८७ || and || ९०). Both verses are there.
  • Paṭala 13, verse numbers 66: The printed edition skips the number 66 (65 is followed by 67). No text is missing: the commentary counts six verses for this run.
  • Paṭala 14, verse numbers 55: The number wraps onto the next line in the e-text. The verse is there.
  • Paṭala 14, verse numbers 65: The edition jumps from 64 to 66. The typists noted this: "numbering switches from 64 directly to 66 in text".
  • Paṭala 19, verse numbers 49: One couplet carries two numbers (|| ४८ || ४९ ||). The next line is a row of asterisks, which the edition uses to mark lost text.
  • Paṭala 29, verse numbers 29: Printed as || . २९ || with a stray dot. The verse is there.
  • Paṭala 32, verse numbers 73: Printed with one bar in front of the number. The verse is there.
  • Paṭala 34, verse numbers after 27: The commentary says one verse after verse 27 has fallen out of the text (अस्मिन् पटले सप्तविंश्लोकात् परं एकः श्लोकः पतितः). The numbers run on with no gap.
